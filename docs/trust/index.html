Why you should not trust the tower

BadTower provides bounded transport behavior without becoming an endpoint, identity, cryptographic, protocol, topology, governance, execution, or final-delivery authority. Every deployment remains untrusted from an endpoint's perspective.

A Station Signal never proves what matters to you

Any reservation result, admission result, claim, time, capacity state, settlement, health, or delivery statement produced by a Station is a Station Signal. Its trust meaning belongs to the caller's own protocol and endpoint model — never to the Station. A Station Signal can guide service-local retry or operation, but it cannot prove:

  • peer identity,
  • authenticity,
  • integrity,
  • freshness,
  • replay rejection,
  • user approval,
  • endpoint receipt,
  • task completion,
  • tool effect, or
  • business acknowledgement.

Settlement is not an endpoint receipt. Readiness is not endpoint reachability. Delivery statements are not delivery evidence.

Observation must not expose you

Station observation is bounded, low-cardinality, and privacy-minimal. It must not expose payloads, ciphertext, endpoint identifiers, protocol content, keys, credentials, private addresses, machine identity, or raw runtime state. Package and plugin verification receipts likewise carry only public identity, version, digest, byte count, and verification key ID.

"Opaque" means the Station cannot read the payload

In the Go Station, the Core treats Transport Unit payloads as opaque bytes: public and persistent schemas contain no protocol, endpoint, or product-specific field, and a plugin may parse the minimum external surface it owns without transferring that meaning into Core. In BadTower.js the Worker routes 32-byte delivery handles to mailboxes and never sees plaintext, identities, sessions, or sizes beyond packet bytes — the bound subject is one fixed-size opaque identifier, and the core does not know it is an Ed25519 key.

Endpoints therefore keep their own trust work: keys, encryption and decryption, identity proof, approval, replay decisions, local effects, and final evidence all stay outside BadTower's ownership. Every client treats Station Signals according to its own trust model.

Admission is invitation-gated in BadTower.js

A mailbox exists only after the first valid btm1 admission credential binds. Credentials are minted offline by the operator's station tooling; the credential's notAfter bounds only the first binding, after which a bound subject stays admitted until revocation. The only operator endpoint on the Station itself is POST /v1/revoke, which verifies a fixed-size offline-HMAC body — there is no operator service or account surface.

Honest reading: these are design properties of the transport boundary, not a hosted-service guarantee, certification, or proof that any particular Station is operated honestly. No named public Station is claimed to exist.
